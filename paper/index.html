Prioritizing Content Pages at Risk of Search-Visibility Decline

A leakage-controlled ranking approach for deciding which pages a human reviewer should inspect first.

Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring90-day historical features30-day future outcome
01 · ABSTRACT

From noisy search data to a review queue

This project asks which content pages should be prioritized for human review when the goal is to identify pages that subsequently experience a defined decline in search visibility. A 90-day historical feature window was constructed from March 3 through May 31, 2026, and a future June outcome window was used to define an operational decline label from Google Search Console impressions. Logistic Regression was evaluated with five-fold client-grouped cross-validation and compared with a frozen Week-4 rule-based baseline using Precision@K. On the evaluated population, the model achieved Precision@20 = 0.75 versus 0.45 for the baseline, and Precision@50 = 0.72 versus 0.62. The resulting model scores rank pages for review, while transparent baseline signals provide reason context and proposed human-review actions.

02 · INTRODUCTION

The decision

Content teams can have more pages to review than they can realistically inspect at once. The practical problem is therefore not simply whether a page is declining; it is which pages should be reviewed first.

This capstone frames the problem as ranking. The model produces a score for each eligible client-content pair, pages are ordered by that score, and Precision@20 and Precision@50 measure how concentrated the defined decline outcome is near the top of the queue.

Prioritize human review of content pages showing elevated risk of the defined future visibility-decline outcome.This is decision support, not automatic content editing.

04 · METHODOLOGY

Historical signals → future outcome → ranked review

01

Feature construction

90-day aggregates include impressions, clicks, sessions, pageviews, users, engagement, active days, CTR, average position, content age, update recency, search volume, word count, content type and intent.

02

Operational label

A page is labeled declining when May impressions are at least 100 and May→June impression change is ≤ −40%.

03

Baseline

A frozen rule assigns points for stale content (+3), historical visibility (+2), and high search demand (+1), then ranks by the resulting score.

04

Validation

Five-fold GroupKFold is performed by client_hash_id. Clients do not overlap between train and test within a fold. OOF probabilities provide held-out scores for every observation.

05

Leakage control

June-derived outcome columns are excluded from X. Imputation, scaling and categorical encoding are fitted inside the model pipeline rather than globally.

06

Decision metric

Precision@20 and Precision@50 measure how many of the top-ranked pages are members of the defined future-decline class.

06 · LIMITATIONS

What this work cannot claim

Not Google's algorithm

The model learns associations with the defined GSC impression-decline outcome. It does not identify Google's ranking factors.

Not causal

A model score does not establish that changing a page will cause visibility to recover.

Not long-horizon validation

The experiment uses one future June outcome window. It is not a multi-period walk-forward study.

Not automatic action

The queue prioritizes human review. It does not authorize rewriting, publishing, deleting, merging, or pruning content.

Not universal performance

Reported Precision@K is specific to this dataset, population, label definition and validation design.
