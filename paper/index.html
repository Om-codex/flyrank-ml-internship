Prioritizing Content Pages at Risk of Search-Visibility Decline

A leakage-controlled ranking approach for deciding which content pages a human reviewer should inspect first.

Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring 90-day historical features 30-day future outcome Client-grouped validation
01 · ABSTRACT

From noisy search data to a review queue

This project asks which content pages should be prioritized for human review when the goal is to identify pages that subsequently experience a defined decline in search visibility. A 90-day historical feature window was constructed from March 3 through May 31, 2026, and a future June outcome window was used to define an operational decline label from Google Search Console impressions. Logistic Regression was evaluated using five-fold client-grouped cross-validation and compared with a frozen Week-4 rule-based baseline using Precision@K. On the evaluated population, the model achieved Precision@20 = 0.75 versus 0.45 for the baseline, and Precision@50 = 0.72 versus 0.62. The resulting model scores are used to rank pages for review, while transparent baseline signals supply reason context and proposed human-review actions.

02 · INTRODUCTION / PROBLEM STATEMENT

The decision

Content teams can have more pages to review than they can realistically inspect at once. The practical problem is therefore not simply whether a page is declining; it is which pages should be reviewed first.

This capstone frames the problem as a ranking task. The model produces a score for each eligible client-content pair, pages are ordered by that score, and Precision@20 and Precision@50 measure how concentrated the defined decline outcome is near the top of the queue.

The objective is not to automate editorial decisions. The objective is to make a large content inventory easier to prioritize for human investigation.

DECISION SUPPORTED Prioritize human review of content pages showing elevated risk of the defined future visibility-decline outcome. The ranking is decision support, not an automatic content editing or publishing system.

04 · METHODOLOGY

Historical signals → label → validation → ranked review

01

Feature construction

A 90-day historical feature table was constructed from search and content signals including impressions, clicks, sessions, pageviews, users, engagement, active days, CTR, average position, content age, update recency, search volume, word count, content type and intent.

02

Operational label

A page is labeled declining when May impressions are at least 100 and the May-to-June impression change is less than or equal to −40%.

03

Frozen baseline

The Week-4 baseline assigns points for stale content, historical visibility and high search demand. The resulting baseline score is used as the rule-based ranking reference.

04

Grouped validation

Five-fold GroupKFold validation was performed using client_hash_id as the grouping variable. Clients do not overlap between training and test observations within a fold.

05

Leakage control

June-derived outcome information is excluded from model features. Preprocessing is fitted within each training fold so information from held-out clients does not enter the fitted preprocessing stage.

06

Ranking metric

Precision@20 and Precision@50 measure how many of the highest-ranked pages belong to the defined future-decline class.

Model and baseline have different jobs. The model determines the ranking order. The transparent baseline provides human-readable context for why a page may deserve review.

06 · LIMITATIONS & HONEST FRAMING

What this work cannot claim

Not Google's algorithm

The model learns associations with the defined GSC impression-decline outcome. It does not identify Google's ranking factors or reproduce Google's ranking system.

Not causal

A high model score does not establish that changing a page will cause search visibility to recover.

Not long-horizon validation

The experiment uses the specified June future outcome window. It is not a multi-period walk-forward study across many future months.

Not automatic action

The queue prioritizes human review. It does not automatically authorize rewriting, publishing, deleting, merging or pruning content.

Not universal performance

The reported Precision@K values are observed under this dataset, population, label definition and validation design.

Not guaranteed traffic recovery

The analysis does not estimate causal traffic recovery from any proposed editorial action. Such claims would require controlled experimentation.

07 · RANKED RECOMMENDATIONS

From model score to human action

The model determines order. The transparent baseline supplies reason context. The resulting queue is a human-review workflow rather than an automatic content optimizer.

REVIEW REVIEW BEFORE REVERT

Inspect the relevant visibility signal, check search intent and recent changes, then form a hypothesis before making any content change.

VERIFY VERIFY THEN REVIEW

Large movements should first be checked for measurement, tracking, campaign or other unusual effects before editorial intervention.

MONITOR MONITOR ONLY

Pages showing durable positive movement should not be changed simply because they receive a high model score.

INVESTIGATE INVESTIGATE QUIET RISK

When the model ranks a page highly but the transparent baseline does not provide a strong explanation, send the page to human investigation rather than assuming the model explains the cause.

Queue policy. A model score tells the reviewer where to look; it does not tell the reviewer what definitely caused the decline. Every recommendation remains subject to human review before an editorial decision.
